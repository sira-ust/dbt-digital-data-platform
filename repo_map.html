ust_digital_platform

A read-only analytics warehouse over six source schemas: the MySQL system-event log, the JDA / Blue Yonder WMS replica, the NAV ERP (customers, items, posted invoices, targets, margin history), weekly Mentionlytics social listening, the monthly promo workbook, and a geocode table. Data flows staging → intermediate → dims & facts → reporting marts. Offline LLM scripts write tables that dbt reads, so the dbt build itself never calls a model and stays deterministic.

In business terms: what customers do in our apps, what's in the warehouse, what we invoice and what people say online, turned into the sales reps' voice assistant, a demand-vs-supply report and a weekly trending board.

Social listening → the weekly trending board

Eleven boxes covering thirteen dbt models, showing the table flow only. Five of the boxes are built from a source that an offline Python script writes. That script, and any LLM call it makes, is shown as an inset panel inside the box: it is how the table got its data, not a separate stop the data visits. Since 2026-10 the board a reader sees is int_social_trend_board, which ranks products rather than spellings and has staying power. The weekly ranking is one input to it.

Nine time windows, independent of each other. Windows 1–2 make the weekly numbers; 6–9 make the board.

int_social_concept_mentions new

Every dish or product name a post mentions, cleaned, across all history.

How: one row per (mention, class, name), from unnest(mentioned_dishes) → dish and unnest(mentioned_products) → item. 5
int_social_concept_canon ← stg_mentionlytics__concept_canon new

One product, one name. Every spelling, script and board a thing was named under resolves to a single product.

How: the name → product map. Each (concept_class, concept_norm) gets one product_key (the merge key), one product_name for display, and one product_class. The class is item or dish, by its spellings' votes weighted by posts, with ties going to item. Names with the same words in a different order are merged here in SQL, with no LLM. A name that hasn't been canonicalised yet has no row and ranks as itself. Measured 2026-10-05: Lay's ice cream had been split across 9+ names on both boards.

offline LLM canonicalize_concepts.py, run twice MODEL_SERVING
The script reads box #4 and writes social.concept_canon, and dbt rebuilds this box after each pass:
  1. names → products, each name asked once and new names only.
  2. --consolidate: products whose names share most of their words are asked "same product?" in small star-shaped groups. It never merges a branded product with an unbranded one, after the first run tried "fresh eggs" → "cp fresh eggs".

7
int_social_concept_trends weekly ranking

Ranks each calendar week's dishes and items on two separate boards, by product. This is windows 1–2.

One product before counting: every name is replaced by its canonical product from box #5 before anything is counted. That merges across scripts and across the two boards, so a post naming two spellings counts once (assert_social_merged_product_counts_each_post_once).

Then, per calendar week: aggregate, score, then row_number() partitioned by (week, class). The first week ever collected, and the week in progress, are dropped as incomplete. Read movement, not volume: mention_share is a product's share of that week's mentions, so it survives a change in how much the feed collected.

Why it ranks, not just how strongly: subject_mentions (posts about it) vs ingredient_mentions (posts that cook with it) give demand_type. It is talked_about at a subject share of ≥ 0.5 and cooked_with at ≤ 0.3, with mixed between. Soy sauce sits at 5% (pure ingredient demand) and nam phrik at 78% (genuine product interest).

trend_score = distinct_authors × (1 + ln(1+engagement_norm)/5 + ln(1+views_norm)/10) × single_channel_dampener -- 0.85 if one platform
Reads the resolver's grouping from the previous run. This model also refs stg_mentionlytics__concept_resolution (box #10) for group_primary. That is a dbt source, so there is no cycle, but the reader runs before the writer in the job. Any new resolution column read here needs a one-line ALTER TABLE … ADD COLUMNS first. It bit on the v6 deploy, 2026-08-20.

int_social_trend_board the board new

The trending board with staying power: who is on it, in what order, and with what status. This is windows 6–9.

Why it exists: ranked one week at a time, a viral product disappeared the week its posting slowed. Measured 2026-10-05, 63% of the item board changed every week and an item stayed 1.6 weeks on average. At the shipped settings, churn is ~17%, the board holds ~28 items, and an item stays ~4.5 weeks.

How: one row per (week, class, product) for every product ever on the board or the Rising list, from its first appearance onwards, including weeks with no posts. faded_score is the sum of each past week's share of the board's trend score × 0.5^(normal weeks since / 2). The feed changed size three times in 2026 for reasons that aren't trends, and shares keep those weeks comparable. Then the fast lane, the stay-on rule, low-coverage pauses, and the Rising list are applied.

Stockable first, on the item board: branded and shelf products (box #6) are ranked as their own scope, ahead of every generic category, which gets smaller slots (top 8, stay-rank 10) after them (assert_social_board_shelf_before_generic). The dish board ranks on buzz alone.

new · first week of a run rising · share ≥ 1.25× last week steady cooling · quiet week, or under half its peak

9
int_social_product_variants new

"Which Pepsi? Which Magnum?" For every board product each week, the related products behind it.

How: for a branded product, products of the same brand; otherwise, longer names that contain its whole name. Each comes with its posts over the last 4 weeks, up to 5 per row, and becomes top_variants on the mart. It is deterministic on purpose: an LLM "family / variant" label was tried and grouped one family several ways depending on batch order. It's an evidence list, not a hierarchy, so every product still ranks on its own. The window is recent on purpose: Pepsi's Dubai-chocolate posts ran late July to Aug 24, so the Sep 21 row shows that week's brand campaign, not July's flavour.

stg_mentionlytics__concept_resolution

The AI's answer for each board product: do we sell it, or something close?

How: a view exposing concept_norm → matched_prtnum, recommended_prtnums, rejected_prtnums, match_confidence and group_primary. It casts and dedupes (the latest resolved_at per concept wins, because the source is append-only).

offline LLM resolve_trending_concepts.py MODEL_SERVING
SKU matching follows the board. The script resolves this week's top-N of each class plus every product on the board (box #8), so a product held on through a quiet week still gets its "do we carry it" answer. Each one comes with 28 days of real posts from box #3 (window 5) and the active catalog. Carried stock wins, even for a whole dish. A second, independent pass reviews every candidate against its real catalog name and throws out what it can't defend. Answers are checked against the catalog, so they can't name a product that doesn't exist. Only products not yet resolved are sent.

Search demand: what our own customers type new joins the board in the export
int_events_decoded→int_search_queries→int_search_terms_weekly·int_search_product_weekly⇢analyses/social_trending_by_week.sql

Social listening captures what the market talks about, and almost none of it comes from anyone who buys from us. Search is the opposite: every row is an account we invoice, typing what it wants into our own search box. There are 114,901 searches since 2026-01-19 (83,263 customers on web or app, 31,638 reps building an order) and 24,902 distinct terms, against the 364 concepts the resolver has ever named. The board export puts web_search, pda_ipad_search, ust_accounts and searched_as beside each row. On the newest week, 9 rows have mentions, 16 have searches, and only 4 have both.

  • Reads int_events_decoded, not _enriched, deliberately. Enriched filters sales_code not in ('000'), and '000' is what a customer event carries. That filter plus the SQL NULL trap would drop 144,625 of 247,417 searches, and every one of them comes from a customer.
  • Accounts, not searches, is the demand number. 'mama' is 56 searches from 20 accounts. One pasted SKU line is 260 searches from a single account. Rank on ust_accounts.
  • The join goes through the canon map. The board stores 'mama instant noodles', but customers type 'mama'. Joined on raw text, 2 rows in 20 match. Through int_social_concept_canon, 54 matched terms become 418. The bridge fans out 2× silently, because it is keyed on (class, name), so both sides join on both key parts.
  • The distinct count is taken once per product. Summing ust_accounts across a product's spellings counts an account once per spelling it tried. That overstates 271 of 8,735 product-weeks ('spring rolls' sums to 4 accounts against a true 2). This is why int_search_product_weekly exists.
  • A blank is not a zero. A blank means the search log doesn't cover that week. A 0 means it does, and nobody searched. A fallback name match fills in only where canon has no row, which recovered Thai Coffee (Oliang): 17 mentions and 10 accounts, previously shown as zero.

Rep Voice AI: the sales agent's tables

The UST Sales Agent is a voice assistant a sales rep talks to. It signs in as that rep and answers by querying Unity Catalog tables in ust_databricks on the rep's behalf. The tables below were built for it from the Draft UST Sales Agent Questions (2026-09-08). Each one answers a question a rep actually asks, carries every name beside its code so nothing needs a join to be said out loud, and carries a coverage column wherever a number can be incomplete. The full coverage write-up is models/docs/_sales_agent_tables.md.

How a question gets answered
1 · who is asking

Login → sales_code

dim_rep_logins where username = :login. This is many-to-one and always safe. Admins come back with a null sales_code, which is distinct from "unknown login".

2 · which account

Spoken name → customer_key

Look the name up in dim_customers, scoped to the rep's own book first. One match: proceed. Several: ask, using address and owner rep. None: say so, rather than guessing at the nearest string.

3 · the question

Pick the mart

Filter on sales_code, and on period_type and is_current for the period tables. The call list and pitch list come pre-ranked: order by attention_priority, opportunity_rank.

4 · before speaking

Read the clock & coverage

Say which date the answer is true as of (as_of_date). Check has_value / value_coverage before quoting money, and say which reason backs a suggestion.

Six things to know before quoting any number

Invoice dollars start 2026-09-20

ADF began exporting line amounts that day, and history is never revisited: 19.65M of 19.70M invoice lines will never carry an amount. Counts, dates and quantities are complete throughout. The exception is stg_nav__sales_person_margin_history, the only dollar history reaching back to 2025-01-02, which is why targets and prior-year comparisons use it.

Nothing is coalesced to zero

A line with no exported amount has no value, not $0. A zero would average into every revenue figure as though the goods were given away. Read has_value, value_coverage, valued_purchase_days or return_rate_is_measurable first.

There are four clocks, on four dates

rep_log_today() (the app event log, yesterday) · nav_posted_today() (posted invoices, which lag) · the margin history's own posting edge · stock_snapshot_date (the WMS, last night). They advance independently. Every table publishes its own as_of_date, so say which one you used.

Orders and invoices never tie out

fct_orders is what a rep submitted (app channels, same day). fct_invoices is what was invoiced (every channel including phone and email, full history, lagging). Orders get cut, short-shipped or posted days later. An order keyed on the 30th is in March's orders and April's sales, and neither is wrong.

A customer name is not a key

222 of 3,742 active customers share a name. ASIAN MARKET is 22 different accounts, and LEE'S SANDWICHES in San Jose is 3. Normalising punctuation makes this worse. Every mart carries city, county, post_code and owner_rep_name for disambiguation. Rep codes have no such problem.

Absent is not zero, and period tables repeat

is_in_stock null means the WMS doesn't cover the item, not that it's out of stock. has_store_type false means unknown. And always filter period_type: day / week / month / quarter / year all live in one table, and summing without that filter counts the same sales five times.

Who should I call?

"Who's gone quiet? I'm at ABC Market. Who else round here needs a visit?"
mart_rep_account_status VOICE
ust_reporting · rep × account · clocks: as_of_date (orders) + invoice_as_of_date (NAV)

The call list. The spine unions two senses of "my account": accounts NAV says the rep owns, and accounts the rep has actually sold to. An owned account that has never ordered is exactly what a to-do list exists to surface. Silence is measured on posted invoices, so an account that buys by phone no longer reads as "no order on record".

attention_reason
One cause, in precedence order: no order on record · order overdue · ok · owned by another rep · inactive account.
attention_priority
1 / 2 / 5 / 8 / 9, mirroring the reason. order by it gives the list.
needs_attention
priority ≤ 2: the two reasons that are genuinely the rep's to-do.
last_invoiced_date
Latest posted invoice for this rep-account. Every channel, full history.
delivery_days
"Tue, Thu", so the call lands before the cut-off. A count of 0 means unknown.
rhythm_status WIP
The store's own buying rhythm, across every rep: on rhythm / due / lapsing / former regular. Only a regular (6+ invoice days a year) gets one.
days_past_usual
Days quiet ÷ the store's median gap. 1.0 is exactly its usual wait, so one sort serves weekly and quarterly buyers alike.
rhythm_priority
1 lapsing · 2 due · 3 on rhythm · 4 former regular · 5 other. Independent of attention_priority.
The rhythm columns come from int_customer_invoice_rhythm, uncommitted on feat/account_buying_rhythm. It exists because a rep's own invoice columns measure the relationship, not the shop: PYONE CHO ASIA MARKET has 32 invoices under code 028 and none under 032, and 17 of one rep's 129 owned accounts look like they've never bought anything.
mart_rep_nearby_accounts VOICE
ust_reporting · rep × anchor account × nearby account needing attention

A "while you're there" list, not a neighbour list. It carries only accounts the call list has already flagged (never ordered, or overdue) within 5 km straight-line, ranked by urgency, then distance. Both sides stay inside the same rep's book. Every account can be an anchor, because the assistant doesn't know today's route.

nearby_rank
1 = suggest first. Urgency before proximity.
distance_km
Great-circle distance, 2 dp, for a spoken answer.
attention_reason
Carried through from mart_rep_account_status.
delivery_days
A Thursday-only store isn't worth a Friday detour.

What should I sell them?

"What do they buy? What have they stopped buying? What should I talk about when I walk in?"
mart_account_item_history VOICE
ust_reporting · account × item they have bought · clocks: NAV + stock snapshot

What this account buys, what it has stopped buying, and what it's due to reorder on its own rhythm. A restaurant buying fish sauce every 3 weeks and a grocery buying it every 3 months are both healthy, and a fixed "overdue after N days" rule slanders one of them. This also answers the business's "quarterly vs monthly customer" note.

reorder_status
lapsed / overdue / due / not due / rhythm unknown. 'lapsed' is the answer to "what have they stopped buying".
median_days_between_purchases
The account's own rhythm for the item. Null below 3 purchases: an honest "unknown" beats a fabricated rhythm.
days_overdue
Signed. A negative value means due in that many days, which is itself a reason to call.
avg_quantity_per_purchase
A typical reorder, in the account's own UOM (UOM is part of the grain: 6 CASE ≠ 6 EA).
is_in_stock
Three-valued. Null means the WMS (CABOT only) doesn't cover the item.
Built from int_customer_item_purchases (posted invoice lines netted against credit memos, customer × item × day × UOM) → int_customer_item_cadence (the per-pair rhythm).
mart_account_item_opportunities VOICE
ust_reporting · account × candidate item, ranked, capped at account_item_opportunity_top_n

The pitch list: "the 5–10 items I should walk in and talk about". Every row carries its own reason and the evidence for it, because a voice agent reading a bare ranked list will invent a justification. Discontinued items stay on the list but are flagged. Blocked or out-of-stock items are excluded.

opportunity_reason
In precedence order: reorder due · stopped buying · on promotion · peers buy it · new or featured.
is_evidence_based
True means this account's own purchases justify it ("you're due for this"). False means it rests on others ("you might try this"), so say it differently.
opportunity_evidence
Already phrased for a person: "bought 7 times, last 68 days ago, usually every 31".
has_promo_offer
Buy-X-get-Y is set: "buy 6 get 1 free until the 30th" (promo_end_date).

Supporting dimensions

join on these instead of carrying bare codes
dim_rep_logins VOICE
ust_dims · one row per login (username)

The table an authenticated app hits first: whose data is this person's? It resolves to a sales_code and carries interface_locale for choosing a reply language. Never join it to a metric on sales_code: that's one-to-many and multiplies the metric by the rep's login count. Use dim_reps.

dim_reps VOICE
ust_dims · one row per sales territory code

The rep as an analytical entity, and safe to join to any rep mart without fan-out. The spine is the union of admin logins and NAV account owners, because a new code can own accounts before its login exists. has_login / owns_accounts say which case a row is.

dim_customers VOICE
ust_dims · one row per customer_key, every account

The name to say out loud, the owner rep, whether the account is live, delivery days, store type and coordinates. It includes inactive and un-geocoded accounts: 12% have no resolvable address, and for those has_coordinates = false means a visit is not knowable, not that it didn't happen. ZZZ*/office rows are flagged is_test_account.

dim_items VOICE
ust_dims · one row per item_no (= WMS prtnum = web sku)

The NAV catalog joined to the WMS warehouse view for the first time. item_label is the name to read out. It also carries category, list price, live promotion and offer, lifecycle flags, and stock. The spine is NAV: an item shipping from an unmirrored site is real and sellable with no WMS row (has_wms_item). is_new_now and is_focus_now are false for every item until NAV maintains those windows.

Data quality: dq_* models

These are distinct from the YAML and singular gate tests that block a build (PK, not-null, accepted-values, relationships). The dq_* models run on every build but never block it: they are queryable bad-row and drift tables. The decision rule: if it should block the pipeline, it's a test; if it's ongoing drift to monitor or quarantine, it's a dq_ model.
